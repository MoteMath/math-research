## For Mathematicians

Let $U(x) = x + \mathrm{lowbit}(x)$ and $D(x) = x - \mathrm{lowbit}(x)$ be the Fenwick tree update and query operators (bridged to hardware `x &&& (-x)` via `lowbit_bitvec_eq`). Naive composition of $L$ LSB Mealy transducers over $\{U, D\} ^ *$ produces $3 ^ L$ states. We build a 4-rule **Fenwick Trigger-Chain Compiler** (`compileTC`) that compiles any word $w \in \{U, D\} ^ *$ into an exact LSB Mealy transducer (`evalWord_mod_eq_compile`) and prove **Myhill-Nerode minimality** (`compileTC_minimal`): no LSB transducer with fewer states can compute $w$ modulo $2 ^ n$ for all $n$.

This machinery discharges four exact infinite laws: (1) the **Free-vs-Nilpotent Monoid Dichotomy** — $\{U, D\} ^ *$ acts faithfully as a *free monoid* on unbounded $\mathbb{N}$ (`fenwick_free_monoid`), yet modulo $2 ^ w$ collapses to a finite monoid of exact cardinality $2 ^ w$ (`fenwick_finite_monoid_card`); (2) **Universal Ancestor-Spine Collapse** (`evalWord_D_spine`, `evalWord_U_spine`), showing every word beginning with $D$ or $U$ collapses onto the pure $D$- or $U$-spine; (3) **Two's-Complement Word-Conjugation Duality** (`evalWord_twos_complement_duality`); and (4) **Exact Fenwick Depth Duality** (`fenwick_depth_duality`), relating update depth to popcount and trailing zeros.

## For Non-Mathematicians

Fenwick trees are one of the most widely used data structures in computing, jumping up and down a binary index by adding or subtracting its lowest set bit. We prove that any chain of these jumps can be compiled into the smallest possible bit-by-bit machine, that no two different jump chains ever do the same thing on all whole numbers, yet on $w$-bit computer words there are *exactly* $2 ^ w$ distinct jump behaviors in total—and flipping signs in two's complement swaps upward and downward jumps symmetrically.

## Main Theorems

| Theorem (in `Challenge.lean`) | Statement |
|---|---|
| `evalWord_mod_eq_compile` | For every Fenwick word $w \in \{U, D\} ^ *$, width $n$, and $x \in \mathbb{N}$, `evalWord w x % 2 ^ n` equals the compiled LSB Mealy transducer `(compileTC w).evalMod n x`. |
| `compileTC_minimal` | No LSB Mealy transducer with fewer than `(compileSpec w).1.length + 1` states can compute `evalWord w x % 2 ^ n` for all $n, x \in \mathbb{N}$ (exact Myhill-Nerode minimality). |
| `fenwick_free_monoid` | Two Fenwick words $w _ 1, w _ 2 \in \{U, D\} ^ *$ induce the same function on all of $\mathbb{N}$ if and only if $w _ 1 = w _ 2$. |
| `fenwick_finite_monoid_card` | Modulo $2 ^ w$, the monoid generated by $U$ and $D$ has exact cardinality $2 ^ w$ for every width $w$. |
| `evalWord_D_spine` | Any word starting with $D$ collapses to an iterate of $D$ and satisfies $\mathrm{evalWord}(D :: v, x) \mathbin{\&} x = \mathrm{evalWord}(D :: v, x)$. |
| `evalWord_U_spine` | Any word starting with $U$ either vanishes or collapses to a positive iterate of $U$ bracketing $x$ with $\mathrm{evalWord}(U :: v, x) \mathbin{\&} x = D(\mathrm{evalWord}(U :: v, x))$. |
| `evalWord_twos_complement_duality` | Conjugating `evalWord w` by two's-complement negation modulo $2 ^ n$ equals `evalWord (dualWord w)` modulo $2 ^ n$. |
| `fenwick_depth_duality` | For all $0 < x < 2 ^ n$, $\mathrm{depthU}(x, 2 ^ n) = \mathrm{popcount}(2 ^ n - x)$ and $\mathrm{depthU}(x, 2 ^ n) + \mathrm{popcount}(x) = n + 1 - \mathrm{ctz}(x)$. |

## Proof Architecture

1. **LSB Mealy transducer chassis (`LsbTransducer.run_compose`, `evalMod_eq_of_sim`):** provides compositional simulation and bisimulation lifting modulo $2 ^ n$ for all $n$.
2. **Trigger-chain compiler & minimality (`evalWord_mod_eq_compile`, `compileTC_minimal`):** collapses arbitrary words via 4 local trigger rules and proves linear-size Myhill-Nerode optimality.
3. **Free-vs-finite dichotomy (`fenwick_free_monoid`, `fenwick_finite_monoid_card`):** separates unbounded $\mathbb{N}$ (free monoid) from `BitVec w` (exact size $2 ^ w$).
4. **Spine collapse & dualities (`evalWord_D_spine`, `evalWord_U_spine`, `evalWord_twos_complement_duality`, `fenwick_depth_duality`):** proves universal bitwise projection, two's-complement conjugation, and depth identities.
